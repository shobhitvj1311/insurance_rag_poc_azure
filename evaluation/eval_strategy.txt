I'd actually suggest a different order — don't upgrade the embedding model right away, and don't go full factorial across every combination. Here's why, and what I'd do instead.

Why not "upgrade to large immediately"

Jumping to text-embedding-3-large before measuring anything skips the opportunity to show a real before/after comparison — and for a capstone in front of leadership, "we tested it and it was worth the cost" is a much stronger slide than "we assumed the bigger one was better." It also compounds cost and rebuild time into every subsequent experiment before you know if it's even needed.

Why not full 2×2 across every generative model

If you cross retrieval mode (dense/hybrid) × reranking (on/off) × embedding model × N generative models, that's 2 × 2 × 2 × N full evaluation runs — 8 runs per generative model candidate, each taking ~25-30 minutes against 50 questions. That's expensive in both time and API cost, and most of it is wasted: context precision and context recall don't depend on the generation model at all. Those two RAGAS metrics only compare retrieved chunks against the reference answer — the chat model under test never enters that calculation. So there's no need to re-run the retrieval matrix once per generative model; retrieval quality is decided independently.

What I'd actually suggest: a staged, sequential design

Optimize one variable at a time, carrying the winner forward, rather than a full grid:

Stage 1 — Retrieval matrix (4 runs). Hold embedding model at text-embedding-3-small and generation model at gpt-5-mini (your current baseline). Run the dense/hybrid × rerank-on/off matrix you already planned. Pick the winner primarily on context precision and context recall, since that's what these four runs actually differ on.

Stage 2 — Embedding upgrade (1 run). Take the winning retrieval config from Stage 1, rebuild the index with text-embedding-3-large, rerun once. Compare context precision/recall against the Stage 1 winner. If the gain is clear, adopt it; if it's marginal, you now have a defensible reason to stay on small and save the cost — either outcome is a legitimate finding, not a failure.

Stage 3 — Generation model comparison (1-2 runs). Take the winning retrieval + embedding config, hold it fixed, and swap only the chat deployment: gpt-5-mini baseline vs. one or two newer cost-tier candidates. Compare faithfulness and answer relevancy here, since that's what generation quality actually affects.

Total: roughly 6-7 evaluation runs instead of 16+, and each stage answers one specific question cleanly rather than mixing variables together.

Why this is also the better story for leadership

This gives you a clean narrative arc for the presentation: "We optimized retrieval first, then asked whether a better embedding model was worth its cost, then asked whether a newer generation model improved grounded-answer quality — each decision built on the last, holding everything else constant." That's a much easier thing to defend in Q&A than a large comparison table where three things changed at once and it's unclear which one drove the result.
