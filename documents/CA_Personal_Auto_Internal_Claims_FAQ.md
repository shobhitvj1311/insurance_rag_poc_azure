# MAPFRE INSURANCE — CALIFORNIA PERSONAL AUTO CLAIMS
# INTERNAL CLAIMS HANDLER FAQ / JOB AID
### (Reference only — always confirm against the base policy form PA300CA (01/25), applicable endorsements, and the specific policyholder's Declarations before quoting coverage to a customer or third party.)

---

## SECTION 1 — RIDESHARE / DELIVERY DRIVING

**Q1.1 — Caller says they were "driving for Uber/Lyft" when the accident happened. What do I check first?**
Check the Declarations for a **CA-PP-RSE (Transportation Network Company Coverage Endorsement)**. If it's not on the policy, the loss is very likely excluded under Part A Exclusion A.6 (and the parallel exclusions in Parts B, C, and D) — TNC/livery use is excluded by default. If the endorsement IS present, next ask which "Period" the driver was in at the time of loss (see Q1.2). Do not assume coverage just because the customer has "rideshare coverage" in general conversation — confirm the endorsement is attached to the specific vehicle/policy in question.

**Q1.2 — What are "Period 1," "Period 2," and "Period 3" and why does it matter?**
These come from the CA-PP-RSE endorsement, not the base policy:
- Period 1 = app on, waiting for a ride request (no trip accepted yet)
- Period 2 = trip accepted, driving to pick up the rider
- Period 3 = rider is in the vehicle

The endorsement (when purchased) buys back the TNC exclusion for all three periods, but the **limits of liability during Period 1 may be lower** — they're capped at the statutory minimum ($50,000/$100,000 BI, $30,000 PD) unless the Declarations show higher limits. During Periods 2 and 3, this policy is typically **excess** over any insurance the TNC company itself provides. Always pull both the endorsement schedule and the TNC company's own coverage documentation (from the customer or the platform) before finalizing a Period 2/3 liability determination.

**Q1.3 — Caller says they were delivering food for a delivery app (not a rideshare passenger app). Is that the same thing?**
**No — this is a common trap.** Food, grocery, and product delivery is excluded under Part A Exclusion A.7 regardless of whether the customer has a rideshare (CA-PP-RSE) endorsement. The RSE endorsement only buys back the passenger-transport exclusion (A.6); it does **not** touch A.7. If the loss occurred while the insured was actively engaged in delivering food/products for compensation, treat it as excluded and escalate to a supervisor before communicating a denial — this is a frequent source of customer confusion and complaints.

**Q1.4 — How do I confirm which "period" a rideshare driver was in?**
Request the trip log / driver app screenshot from the TNC platform (Uber, Lyft, etc. all provide this on request or via subpoena if needed) showing login status and trip status timestamped to the loss. Do not rely solely on the customer's verbal account.

## SECTION 2 — NAMED DRIVER EXCLUSIONS

**Q2.1 — A driver on the police report is a "named driver exclusion" on the policy. Does that mean no coverage at all?**
Under Form CA-PP-NDE, if the excluded driver was operating the vehicle at the time of loss, there is **no coverage under Part A, B, C, or D** for that occurrence — not just reduced coverage, no coverage. This applies even if the vehicle involved isn't the policy's covered auto (the exclusion follows the driver, not just the car). It does not, however, remove coverage for an innocent passenger's own Medical Payments claim.

**Q2.2 — The named insured says they didn't know the excluded driver was using the car. Does that change anything?**
No. The endorsement excludes coverage "regardless of whether the Named Insured knew of that operation." Permission (or lack of it) is not the deciding factor here — the driver's identity is.

**Q2.3 — Can we just remove the exclusion after a loss to pay the claim?**
No. Endorsement changes are prospective only and require a written request plus the Company's endorsement issuance. An exclusion cannot be retroactively removed to cover a loss that already occurred.

## SECTION 3 — NEWLY ACQUIRED / REPLACEMENT VEHICLES

**Q3.1 — Customer bought a new car recently and hasn't called us yet — is it covered?**
Depends on two things: (1) is it a **replacement** for a vehicle already on the Declarations, or an **additional** vehicle, and (2) how many days have passed.
- **Replacement vehicle:** Covered automatically without the customer having to call, for Liability/Med Pay/UM (Part N definition). BUT for Collision/Comprehensive specifically, the customer still needs to have asked within 14 days if any vehicle on the Dec page already carries that coverage (or 4 days if none do — with a $500 deductible applying if a loss happens before they call).
- **Additional vehicle** (in addition to, not replacing, an existing vehicle): the customer **must** ask within 14 days of becoming the owner for any coverage other than Collision, or coverage doesn't attach for that "newly acquired auto." Same 14-day/4-day Collision rule applies as above.

**Q3.2 — It's been more than 14 days and the customer never called. Now what?**
For an **additional** vehicle past the 14-day window with no request to add it: there is no coverage under this policy for that vehicle — it was never validly added. Confirm the vehicle isn't already listed on the Dec page before making this determination, and check the "newly acquired auto" definition (Part N/O of the base form) carefully — for a **replacement** vehicle, liability/UM/Med Pay coverage can still apply even outside 14 days, since no request is required for those coverages when it's a replacement. Escalate close calls (e.g., 13 vs. 15 days, ambiguous "replacement vs. additional" facts) to a coverage specialist rather than denying outright.

## SECTION 4 — RENTAL REIMBURSEMENT & LOAN/LEASE PAYOFF

**Q4.1 — Customer wants a rental car while their car is being fixed. What do I check?**
Confirm Rental Reimbursement is elected on the Declarations (it is **not** automatic) and that both Comprehensive and Collision Coverage are carried on that vehicle. Pull the specific daily limit and policy maximum from the Endorsements document (CA-PP-RR schedule) — these vary by policy, there is no single standard amount. Reimbursement generally starts once the vehicle is withdrawn from use for repairs (or immediately for a reported theft, after a 48-hour waiting period).

**Q4.2 — What's the difference between the automatic "Transportation Expenses" benefit and purchased "Rental Reimbursement Coverage"?**
Transportation Expenses (base form, Part D) is a **modest, automatic** benefit — up to $20/day, $600 max, with no separate premium — that applies whenever Comp or Collision coverage applies to the loss. Rental Reimbursement is a **separate, optional, purchased** coverage with its own (usually higher) daily/max limits shown in the endorsement schedule. **If Rental Reimbursement applies, it replaces — it does not stack with — the automatic Transportation Expenses benefit**, per the base form's own language ("If Rental Reimbursement Coverage applies, no other coverage under this policy for rental expenses will apply").

**Q4.3 — Vehicle is a total loss and financed/leased — what's "gap" coverage here called and how does it work?**
It's "Loan/Lease Payoff Coverage" in this policy (not called "gap" in the form itself, but that's the industry term customers will use). It only applies if the customer purchased it (check Declarations/Endorsement CA-PP-LLP) and only on top of a Comprehensive or Collision total loss payment. It pays the difference between ACV and the loan/lease payoff balance, minus specific deductions (excess mileage, past-due payments, extended warranty refunds, etc.), and is capped at the percentage of ACV shown in that policy's endorsement schedule — this percentage varies by policy.

## SECTION 5 — UNINSURED / UNDERINSURED MOTORIST (UM/UIM)

**Q5.1 — Customer's Dec page doesn't show a UM Property Damage limit at all. Is that an error?**
Not necessarily — UM Property Damage is a separate opt-in in California, distinct from UM Bodily Injury. Some customers decline the property damage piece while keeping UM BI. Always check the Declarations line item specifically; don't assume BI and PD travel together.

**Q5.2 — Customer says they "waived" UM entirely years ago. How do we confirm that?**
CA law requires a written rejection form signed by the named insured for UM/UIM to be excluded entirely from a personal auto policy. Check the policy file for a signed UM Rejection form. If it's on file and this is the customer's original signature/policy term, no UM coverage of any kind applies, and this should be treated as a firm exclusion, not just a "low limit."

**Q5.3 — Hit-and-run, other driver never identified. What has to happen for this to be a valid UM claim?**
For a hit-and-run, the vehicle has to have made **physical contact** with the insured, a vehicle the insured was in, or the covered auto. Also flag the requirement that a police report be made within 24 hours and that the insured provide the Company a sworn statement within 30 days of the accident — missing these procedural steps is a common reason UM hit-and-run claims get delayed, and can be a barrier to payment if not addressed promptly. Loop in a UM specialist early on any hit-and-run file.

**Q5.4 — Does the collision deductible still apply if the at-fault party is an uninsured motorist?**
Only if the policy has the **Waiver of Collision Deductible (Uninsured Motorist)** provision active (endorsement CA-PP-WCD, or indicated on the Declarations) AND the insured is legally entitled to recover the loss from the uninsured driver. If the customer is only partially at fault or only entitled to recover a percentage, the waiver applies proportionally — it's not all-or-nothing.

## SECTION 6 — HOUSEHOLD RESIDENTS / UNDISCLOSED DRIVERS

**Q6.1 — An undisclosed household member (never reported as a driver) caused an at-fault accident. Do we still owe under Liability?**
Generally yes for third-party Liability (Part A) — the "family member"/household resident definition doesn't require prior disclosure to trigger liability coverage for a third party's claim. However, flag for underwriting review: failure to disclose a household driver is a **Duty to Report Changes** violation and can affect rating retroactively, and under Part A, Exclusion C.1, coverage in excess of state minimum financial responsibility limits can be excluded specifically for an undisclosed household resident/family member operator. Don't deny outright — route to a coverage specialist to apply Exclusion C.1 correctly (it only limits the excess above minimum limits, not the whole claim).

## SECTION 7 — MEDICAL PAYMENTS / DISPUTES

**Q7.1 — A medical provider is disputing our reduced payment on a Med Pay bill. What's our process?**
Under Part B, the Company (not the insured) is responsible for resolving disputes over what is a "usual and customary charge" if a provider disputes our payment — the insured is not responsible for the reduction. Don't ask the customer to pay the difference or negotiate directly with the provider; route the dispute to the Med Pay bill review team.

**Q7.2 — Can the insured sue us directly over an unreasonable/unnecessary medical expense determination?**
Only after they've either paid the full disputed amount to the provider themselves, or the provider has started collection activity against them. This is a specific precondition in the base form — flag it if a customer or their attorney raises a lawsuit threat prematurely.

## SECTION 8 — GENERAL PROCESS REMINDERS

**Q8.1 — Customer wants to know if we can pay their claim in cash or must repair the vehicle.**
Per Part D "Payment of Loss," the Company has the option to pay in money or to repair/replace the property — the choice is the Company's, not the customer's, though this is often negotiated in practice for customer satisfaction.

**Q8.2 — What triggers mandatory escalation to a supervisor or coverage specialist (non-exhaustive)?**
- Any named driver exclusion claim
- Any TNC/rideshare or delivery-driving claim
- Any "newly acquired auto" timing dispute (14-day/4-day windows)
- Any UM claim involving a signed rejection form or a disputed "uninsured motor vehicle" status
- Any claim where an endorsement appears to conflict with the base policy wording
- Any claim near a state-minimum vs. purchased-limit boundary that could trigger Exclusion C.1 (undisclosed operator)

**Q8.3 — Where do I find the actual dollar limits/deductibles/endorsement schedule for a specific policy?**
The base policy form (PA300CA 01/25) never states dollar amounts for optional coverages — those are always on that policy's Declarations page and, where applicable, the policy-specific Endorsement Schedule. Never quote a dollar figure to a customer from memory or from a different policy file — always pull the specific policy number's Declarations and Endorsement Schedule first.
