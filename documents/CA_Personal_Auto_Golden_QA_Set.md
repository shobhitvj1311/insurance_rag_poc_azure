# GOLDEN Q&A EVALUATION SET — CA PERSONAL AUTO CLAIMS RAG ASSISTANT
### 50 questions with correct answers and source citations, for retrieval/answer quality evaluation

**How to use this set:** For each question, check whether the assistant's answer matches the "Correct Answer" in substance and whether it cites the same source document(s)/clause(s). Category tags indicate what kind of retrieval the question exercises:
- **[BASE]** — answerable from the base policy form alone (PA300CA 01/25), no policy-specific data needed
- **[DEC]** — requires the specific policyholder's Declarations page
- **[END]** — requires an Endorsement's standard form text and/or schedule
- **[MULTI]** — requires combining two or more of: base form + Declarations + endorsement (the highest-risk category for misquotes)
- **[FAQ]** — requires the Internal Claims FAQ (operational guidance, not a coverage determination itself)

**Q1. [MULTI]** Tyler Webb is a named-driver-excluded operator on policy CAPA-2026-0001 (Marcus Webb). Tyler borrows the covered Honda Civic without Marcus's knowledge and rear-ends another car, causing bodily injury to the other driver. Is there Liability coverage for this claim?
**Correct Answer:** No. Endorsement CA-PP-NDE (Named Driver Exclusion), attached to this policy with Tyler Webb as the Excluded Driver, eliminates Part A/B/C/D coverage for any claim arising while Tyler operates any vehicle — regardless of whether he had permission or whether the named insured knew. Permission is not the deciding factor here; the driver's identity is.
**Source:** Endorsement CA-PP-NDE, Sections 1–2; Endorsements document, per-policy schedule for CAPA-2026-0001; Internal FAQ Q2.1–Q2.2.

**Q2. [MULTI]** On the same policy (CAPA-2026-0001), if instead Marcus Webb himself (not Tyler) is a passenger injured while Tyler is driving, can Marcus still recover under his own Medical Payments Coverage?
**Correct Answer:** Likely yes for Marcus's own Med Pay claim — the Named Driver Exclusion endorsement (Section 3) states it does not remove the excluded driver as an "insured" for purposes of coverage available to *other* insureds, and Part B Med Pay covers "you" (the named insured) while occupying "your covered auto" regardless of who caused the accident. This is a nuanced interpretation and should be confirmed with a coverage specialist before quoting to the customer, since Section 1's broad exclusion language and Section 3's carve-out can appear to conflict.
**Source:** Endorsement CA-PP-NDE, Sections 1 & 3; Base Form Part B, Insuring Agreement & "Insured" definition B.1.

**Q3. [DEC]** What are the Uninsured Motorists Bodily Injury limits on policy CAPA-2026-0002 (Priya Chandran)?
**Correct Answer:** $50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident (matches her Bodily Injury Liability limits).
**Source:** Declarations, CAPA-2026-0002.

**Q4. [MULTI]** Priya Chandran (CAPA-2026-0002) has a CA-PP-RSE Rideshare endorsement. She was logged into a TNC app in "Period 1" (waiting for a trip, none accepted) when she caused an at-fault accident. What Bodily Injury liability limit applies to this loss?
**Correct Answer:** $50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident — the endorsement caps Period 1 liability at the statutory minimum under Cal. Ins. Code §11580.24(b) unless the Declarations show higher limits, and in this case her Declarations limits are already exactly at that floor, so the result is the same either way.
**Source:** Endorsement CA-PP-RSE, Section 2; Declarations, CAPA-2026-0002.

**Q5. [MULTI]** Derek Simmons (CAPA-2026-0003) was logged into a rideshare app and had accepted a trip (en route to pick up a passenger — "Period 2") when he caused an accident. Does his policy provide Liability coverage?
**Correct Answer:** No. His policy has no CA-PP-RSE (TNC) endorsement attached, so Part A Exclusion A.6 (public/livery conveyance, including TNC platform login) applies without modification.
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Exclusion A.6; Endorsements document confirms no CA-PP-RSE on CAPA-2026-0003.

**Q6. [MULTI]** Alicia Ferreira (CAPA-2026-0004) has a CA-PP-RSE Rideshare endorsement. At the time of the loss, she was making a delivery for a food-delivery app, not transporting a passenger. Is this loss covered?
**Correct Answer:** No. Food/product delivery is excluded under Part A Exclusion A.7 regardless of whether a rideshare endorsement is present — the CA-PP-RSE endorsement only buys back Exclusion A.6 (passenger transport), and its Section 4 expressly states it does not modify or buy back Exclusion A.7. This is a common point of confusion; escalate before communicating a denial.
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Exclusion A.7; Endorsement CA-PP-RSE, Section 4; Internal FAQ Q1.3.

**Q7. [MULTI]** Nathan Osei's parked, unoccupied vehicle (CAPA-2026-0005) was struck and damaged (property damage only, no injuries) by a hit-and-run driver who was never identified. Does UM coverage apply to the vehicle damage?
**Correct Answer:** No. Nathan's Declarations show UM Bodily Injury elected but UM Property Damage NOT elected — Part C's Insuring Agreement A.2 requires the Declarations to indicate that property damage UM coverage applies to that auto, which is not the case here.
**Source:** Declarations, CAPA-2026-0005; Base Form Part C, Insuring Agreement A.2.

**Q8. [MULTI]** Yolanda Reyes (CAPA-2026-0006) is injured by an uninsured motorist. Does Part C apply?
**Correct Answer:** No coverage of any kind under Part C. Yolanda signed a California UM/UIM rejection form at policy inception; her Declarations confirm no UM Bodily Injury or Property Damage coverage exists on this policy at all.
**Source:** Declarations, CAPA-2026-0006 (UM Rejected in Writing); Internal FAQ Q5.2.

**Q9. [MULTI]** Rosa Delgado (CAPA-2026-0007) has two covered vehicles — a RAV4 with a $500 Collision deductible and an Accord with a $1,000 Collision deductible. Both were damaged in the same single-vehicle rollover accident. What deductible(s) apply?
**Correct Answer:** Only one deductible applies — the higher of the two, $1,000 — not both added together. The base form's Deductible provision states that if loss to more than one covered auto results from the same loss, only the highest applicable deductible applies.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, "Deductible" provision; Declarations, CAPA-2026-0007.

**Q10. [MULTI]** Kevin Larsson (CAPA-2026-0008) traded in his prior vehicle for a 2024 Prius 9 days before a collision loss, and had not yet called to report the replacement. His Declarations already show Collision Coverage on the prior (traded-in) vehicle. Is Collision Coverage available for the Prius?
**Correct Answer:** Coverage can apply, but it is conditional: because the Declarations already showed Collision Coverage on at least one prior vehicle, the window to ask for Collision on the replacement "newly acquired auto" is 14 days from the date he became the owner. Only 9 days had passed at the time of loss, so the window had not yet closed — if Kevin (or his agent) asks within the remaining days, Collision coverage attaches retroactively to the date of ownership, covering this loss. If the 14 days lapse without a request, Collision coverage would not attach. Note that other coverages (Liability, Med Pay, UM) attach automatically for a replacement vehicle without needing to ask.
**Source:** Base Form Definitions, Part O ("Newly acquired auto"), O.2.a and O.2.b(1); Declarations & notes, CAPA-2026-0008; Internal FAQ Q3.1.

**Q11. [MULTI]** Michelle Anand (CAPA-2026-0009) bought an additional 2023 Chevrolet Bolt 20 days before an accident, in addition to (not replacing) her Sorento shown on the Declarations. She never called to add it. The Bolt was involved in an at-fault accident causing third-party bodily injury. Is there Liability coverage for the Bolt?
**Correct Answer:** No. Because the Bolt is an *additional* newly acquired auto (not a replacement) and more than 14 days passed without the insured asking to add it, no "newly acquired auto" coverage ever attached to the Bolt. It is not "your covered auto" under this policy, and this policy provides no Liability coverage for it.
**Source:** Base Form Definitions, Part O.2.a (14-day requirement for additional autos); Declarations & notes, CAPA-2026-0009; Internal FAQ Q3.2.

**Q12. [MULTI]** Aaron Whitfield (CAPA-2026-0010) has Rental Reimbursement Coverage with a $40/day, $1,200 maximum schedule. His covered vehicle was in the shop 3 days for a covered collision repair, and he rented a car costing $45/day ($135 total). How much will MAPFRE reimburse?
**Correct Answer:** $120 total ($40/day × 3 days), the policy's daily cap under the CA-PP-RR schedule — not the full $135. He is responsible for the $5/day difference above the cap. The 3-day total is well under the $1,200 policy maximum.
**Source:** Endorsements document, CA-PP-RR schedule, CAPA-2026-0010; Base Form Additional Coverages, Rental Reimbursement Coverage section.

**Q13. [MULTI]** Bianca Costa (CAPA-2026-0011) does not have Rental Reimbursement Coverage, but does carry both Comprehensive and Collision. Her car is in the shop for a covered collision repair and she wants a rental reimbursed at $45/day. What, if anything, is available?
**Correct Answer:** She is not eligible for the optional Rental Reimbursement Coverage (not purchased), but she is entitled to the base policy's automatic Transportation Expenses benefit: up to $20/day, maximum $600, beginning once the vehicle is withdrawn from use for more than 24 hours.
**Source:** Declarations, CAPA-2026-0011 (Rental Reimbursement: No); Base Form Part D, "Transportation Expenses" section; Internal FAQ Q4.2.

**Q14. [MULTI]** Felix Okoro's financed Chevrolet Equinox (CAPA-2026-0012) is declared a total loss after a covered collision. ACV is $18,400 and the loan payoff balance is $22,900 (a $4,500 gap). His Loan/Lease Payoff endorsement caps payment at 25% of ACV. How much (before other standard deductions) can Loan/Lease Payoff pay toward the gap?
**Correct Answer:** Up to $4,500 — the actual gap. The 25% cap on his $18,400 ACV is $4,600, which is higher than the $4,500 gap, so the cap does not reduce the payment in this case (though the base form's standard deductions — unpaid finance charges, excess mileage, extended warranty/credit insurance refunds, past-due payments, collection/repossession expenses — could still reduce the final figure).
**Source:** Endorsement CA-PP-LLP schedule, CAPA-2026-0012; Base Form Additional Coverages, Loan/Lease Payoff Coverage section.

**Q15. [MULTI]** Monica Fairweather's vehicle (CAPA-2026-0013) has a custom lift kit, off-road bumper, and custom wheels (combined replacement cost $6,000), vandalized in a covered comprehensive loss. She has a Custom Equipment endorsement scheduling this equipment at $5,000. What would the cap have been without the endorsement, and what applies with it?
**Correct Answer:** Without the endorsement, Part D Exclusion 12 caps payment for non-manufacturer performance/appearance modifications (explicitly including custom wheels and lift kits) at $1,000. With the CA-PP-CE endorsement scheduling this equipment at $5,000, that higher scheduled limit applies instead — so up to $5,000 is payable (subject to the applicable deductible); the insured absorbs the $1,000 difference between the $6,000 replacement cost and the $5,000 scheduled limit.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, Exclusion 12; Endorsement CA-PP-CE, schedule for CAPA-2026-0013.

**Q16. [MULTI]** Gordon Achebe (CAPA-2026-0014) has Waiver of Collision Deductible coverage. He is struck by an unidentified hit-and-run driver and is legally entitled to recover 100% of the loss from that driver. His Collision deductible is $500. Does the deductible apply?
**Correct Answer:** No. Because the loss qualifies as being caused by an "uninsured motor vehicle" under Items 1/4 of that definition (hit-and-run), the Waiver of Collision Deductible provision is active on this policy (per Declarations/Endorsement CA-PP-WCD), and he is entitled to recover 100% of the loss — so no deductible applies at all.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, "Uninsured Motorist Waiver of Collision Deductible" provision; Endorsement CA-PP-WCD / Declarations, CAPA-2026-0014.

**Q17. [MULTI]** Teresa Blackwood's policy (CAPA-2026-0015) carries 100/300/100 Liability limits — above California's 30/60/15 financial responsibility minimum. Her adult son Connor is a household resident who regularly drives the covered auto but was never disclosed to MAPFRE. Connor caused an at-fault accident resulting in $80,000 of third-party bodily injury damages. How much of that $80,000 is MAPFRE obligated to pay under this policy?
**Correct Answer:** Potentially only up to California's financial responsibility minimum — $60,000 per accident for Bodily Injury — rather than the full $300,000 per-accident limit shown on the Declarations. Part A, Exclusion C.1 excludes coverage, to the extent limits exceed the state minimum, for a family member/household resident operator the carrier was not notified of before the accident. This is fact-sensitive and should be escalated to a coverage specialist rather than resolved by a handler alone.
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Exclusion C.1; Definitions D (minimum limits); Declarations & notes, CAPA-2026-0015; Internal FAQ Section 6.

**Q26. [BASE]** Under Supplementary Payments in Part A, how much will MAPFRE pay toward bail bonds required because of a covered accident?
**Correct Answer:** Up to $250, and only for bail bonds related to an accident resulting in "bodily injury" or "property damage" covered under the policy (including related traffic law violations).
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Supplementary Payments, item 1.

**Q27. [BASE]** What is the maximum daily amount MAPFRE will pay for an insured's lost earnings due to attendance at a hearing or trial at the company's request?
**Correct Answer:** Up to $200 a day (for loss of earnings only, not other income).
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Supplementary Payments, item 4.

**Q28. [BASE]** What is the automatic Transportation Expenses limit under Part D, absent any purchased Rental Reimbursement Coverage?
**Correct Answer:** Up to $20 per day, to a maximum of $600, beginning (for non-theft losses) once the vehicle is withdrawn from use for more than 24 hours, or (for total theft) 48 hours after the theft is reported.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, "Transportation Expenses" section.

**Q29. [BASE]** How long does an insured have to formally institute arbitration proceedings for a Part C property damage dispute?
**Correct Answer:** Within 1 year from the date of the accident. (Note: disputes concerning coverage itself under Part C may not be arbitrated — only whether the insured is legally entitled to recover damages and the amount.)
**Source:** Base Form Part C, Arbitration section.

**Q30. [BASE]** What advance notice must MAPFRE give for cancellation due to nonpayment of premium, versus other cancellation reasons?
**Correct Answer:** At least 10 days' notice for nonpayment of premium; at least 20 days' notice in all other cases. After the policy has been in effect 60 days (or on a renewal), cancellation is further limited to nonpayment, license suspension/revocation of a relevant driver, or material misrepresentation of specific rating factors.
**Source:** Base Form Part F, "Termination — Cancellation."

**Q31. [BASE]** What is the required advance notice if MAPFRE decides not to renew a policy?
**Correct Answer:** At least 30 days before the end of the policy period.
**Source:** Base Form Part F, "Termination — Nonrenewal."

**Q32. [BASE]** If a medical provider disputes the amount MAPFRE paid on a Med Pay claim as not being a "usual and customary charge," who is responsible for resolving that dispute — the insured or MAPFRE?
**Correct Answer:** MAPFRE. The base form states the insured "shall not be responsible for payment of any reduction applied by us," and if a provider disputes the amount, MAPFRE is responsible for resolving the dispute.
**Source:** Base Form Part B, "'Usual and customary charges'" definition, paragraph C.

**Q33. [BASE]** Does this policy provide any coverage for accidents that occur while driving in Mexico?
**Correct Answer:** No. The policy contains an explicit warning that it does not provide coverage in Mexico, and recommends securing coverage from a Mexican-licensed insurer before driving there.
**Source:** Base Form, front-matter Warning notice (page 3).

**Q34. [BASE]** How many consecutive days can an insured possess/operate a rental or borrowed vehicle before it stops qualifying as a "non-owned auto" under Part D?
**Correct Answer:** 30 or more consecutive days (this cutoff does not apply to an authorized temporary substitute vehicle).
**Source:** Base Form Part D, Definitions, "Non-owned auto," D.2.

**Q35. [BASE]** What is the maximum MAPFRE will pay for loss to a "non-owned auto" that is a trailer, under Part D?
**Correct Answer:** $1,500.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, "Limit of Liability," paragraph A.

**Q36. [BASE]** Under "Our Right to Recover Payment," if MAPFRE pursues subrogation against a responsible third party, will it also try to recover the insured's deductible?
**Correct Answer:** Yes, MAPFRE will attempt to recover the insured's deductible as well when it exercises recovery rights against another party — unless the insured specifically instructs MAPFRE not to pursue the deductible.
**Source:** Base Form Part F, "Our Right to Recover Payment," paragraph E.

**Q37. [BASE]** If an insured settles a Part C Uninsured Motorists bodily injury claim on their own, without MAPFRE's consent, and that settlement prejudices MAPFRE's right to recover payment, is coverage affected?
**Correct Answer:** Yes — Part C Exclusion B.1 excludes coverage in that situation, unless the settlement was made with the insurer of an underinsured motor vehicle (item 2 of the "uninsured motor vehicle" definition), in which case the exclusion does not apply.
**Source:** Base Form Part C, Exclusion B.1.

**Q38. [BASE]** Under Part A, is coverage available for "punitive or exemplary damages" awarded against an insured?
**Correct Answer:** No — Part A Exclusion 13 specifically excludes punitive or exemplary damages.
**Source:** Base Form Part A, Exclusion A.13.

**Q39. [BASE]** Can two vehicles' Liability limits on the same policy be "stacked" to increase the total available coverage for one accident?
**Correct Answer:** No. If the policy insures two or more autos (or if another policy from the same company applies to the same incident), the maximum limit of liability is the highest limit applicable to any one auto — limits are never added together, combined, or stacked.
**Source:** Base Form, "Two or More Autos Insured; Two or More Auto Policies" (General Provisions).

**Q45. [MULTI]** Victor Davis (CAPA-2026-0030) carries state-minimum Liability limits (30/60/15) and has Rental Reimbursement elected. His Tesla has Comprehensive Coverage ($100 deductible) but no Collision Coverage. If his Tesla is damaged in a collision with another vehicle (his fault), is Rental Reimbursement available for the repair period?
**Correct Answer:** No. Rental Reimbursement Coverage under the base form requires that the loss be covered under one of Comprehensive or Collision Coverage, and that both be purchased for that vehicle. Since Victor's Tesla has no Collision Coverage at all, a collision loss is not covered in the first place (Comprehensive alone does not cover collision losses), so no physical damage payment — and therefore no Rental Reimbursement — is available for this loss.
**Source:** Declarations, CAPA-2026-0030; Base Form Additional Coverages, Rental Reimbursement Coverage section (requires both Collision and Comprehensive purchased and the loss covered under one of them).

**Q46. [FAQ]** A medical provider is disputing MAPFRE's reduced payment on a Part B Med Pay bill and is asking the insured to pay the difference. What should the claims handler do?
**Correct Answer:** Do not ask the customer to pay the difference or negotiate directly with the provider — under the policy, MAPFRE (not the insured) is responsible for resolving disputes over "usual and customary" charges. Route the dispute to the Med Pay bill review team.
**Source:** Internal Claims FAQ, Q7.1; Base Form Part B.

**Q47. [FAQ]** Name at least three claim types that internal guidance says must always be escalated to a supervisor or coverage specialist rather than resolved by a handler alone.
**Correct Answer:** Any of: named driver exclusion claims; TNC/rideshare or delivery-driving claims; "newly acquired auto" timing disputes (14-day/4-day windows); UM claims involving a signed rejection form or disputed "uninsured motor vehicle" status; claims where an endorsement appears to conflict with base policy wording; claims near a state-minimum vs. purchased-limit boundary that could trigger Exclusion C.1.
**Source:** Internal Claims FAQ, Q8.2.

**Q48. [FAQ]** A customer whose vehicle was damaged in a covered loss insists MAPFRE must pay them cash rather than have the vehicle repaired. Must MAPFRE comply?
**Correct Answer:** No — under Part D "Payment of Loss," the choice of paying in money versus repairing or replacing the property belongs to the Company, not the customer, though in practice this is often negotiated for customer satisfaction.
**Source:** Base Form Part D, "Payment of Loss"; Internal Claims FAQ, Q8.1.

**Q49. [FAQ]** How should a claims handler verify which "period" (1, 2, or 3) a rideshare driver was logged into at the time of loss, rather than relying on the customer's account alone?
**Correct Answer:** Request the trip log or driver-app screenshot from the TNC platform (e.g., Uber, Lyft), timestamped to the time of loss, showing login and trip status — don't rely solely on the customer's verbal account.
**Source:** Internal Claims FAQ, Q1.4.

**Q50. [FAQ]** Where should a claims handler look to confirm the exact dollar amount of a specific policy's Rental Reimbursement daily limit before quoting it to a customer?
**Correct Answer:** The base policy form never states dollar amounts for optional coverages — a handler must pull that specific policy number's Declarations page (and, where applicable, its Endorsement Schedule), not rely on memory or another policy's figures.
**Source:** Internal Claims FAQ, Q8.3.
